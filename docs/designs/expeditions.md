## Problem Statement

Two things Bati cannot do today, and one question about how they fit.

1. **Start a session on impulse.** Home offers one tap to PLAY, but what it starts is a
   *quest*: a prescribed structure of rounds, exercises and rep targets. There is no door for
   "I feel like moving, now, for a while".
2. **Follow a program with objectives.** Asked as an open question; largely already answered
   by a shipped mechanic nobody remembered was there (see What already exists).
3. **Walking, running and cycling** must all be possible, and must earn their place in a
   dark-fantasy RPG rather than sitting beside it as a stopwatch.

## What Makes This Cool

Everything Bati does happens **inside** the walls: you train in the yard, you forge, you farm,
you fight what comes to you. Walking, running and riding are the only things that take the hero
*out*. And the schema already has the hole shaped like that: `exerciseStyles` has four members,
the village has a building for six muscles, one for calisthenics and one for yoga — and
**no building for anything that happens outside the gate**. `cardio` has none of its own (its
eight movements earn through their muscle tags), and `expedition`, the style these three carry,
is born with neither a resource nor a building.

So the reward for going out is not another pile of stone. It is **reach**: the road out of the
village grows with the ground the hero covers.

## Constraints

- Dark-mode only, Tamagui tokens, React Compiler (no manual memo), one writer per piece of
  state, one source per value.
- Offline-first SQLite + Drizzle. A time-mode expedition needs no network and no GPS.
- MIT, F-Droid, `INTERNET` still blocked at the manifest.
- The catalogue's naming convention is established: real movement, fantasy name
  ("Berserker Burpee", "Monk's Mountain Climber"). The bicycle gets one too.
- The economy is already balanced. Boss HP is set per campaign in
  `drizzle/0026_boss_pacing.sql` (The Golem 278 … The Ranger's Journey 1115) and building
  levels top out at 1000 work units. Nothing here may force a re-tune of either.

## Premises (agreed)

1. An expedition is a **session with one exercise**, not a new entity — it inherits XP, the
   journal, the streak and the oaths for free.
2. Walk, run and ride are three seed exercises with fantasy names, carrying a **style of their
   own, `expedition`**. Written first as `style: cardio`, which was wrong and cost a day: that
   style already holds eight movements — burpees, jumping jacks, mountain climbers, high knees,
   bear crawls, jump squats, skater hops, star jumps — across eleven slots of six shipped
   quests, every one of them counted repetitions that have earned boss damage and village
   volume since the app shipped. Zeroing cardio silently stopped all of them, and the suite
   stayed green. Cardio is what leaves you breathless; an expedition is what leaves the walls.
3. The target is optional: a duration, a distance, or nothing. Countdown is the default and is
   cleared with one tap.
4. The outdoor style earns its place in the village. The hole is real but narrower than first
   written: `cardio` has no *building*, yet its eight movements do earn resources through their
   muscle tags. `expedition` is the style that is born with neither, which is what makes it the
   one the road is for.
5. **A time-mode expedition needs no GPS.** Walking for 25 minutes is measured by the clock;
   only a distance target needs a fix. So expeditions ship before the GPS feature — which is
   what the GPS design already said, not a reversal of it. Stated during the session as though
   it overturned that plan; it does not, and the correction is recorded here rather than
   quietly dropped.
6. Oaths already cover "a program with objectives". The dead `goals`, `goal_progress` and
   `scheduled_sessions` tables are an earlier, worse version — they store a progress that oaths
   compute. Delete rather than revive.
7. Adding `distance` to `questTargetTypes` is a trap: `toRepEquivalent` does
   `if (type !== "time") return resultValue`, so 5000 metres would silently become 5000 reps.
   Making it exhaustive is part of the same change.
8. ~~The three activities need different conversion rates.~~ **Superseded by the currency
   decision below**: nothing converts, so there is no rate to set.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent cold read (Claude subagent) broke the first economic decision with arithmetic,
and every number it used was verified against the repo before this doc was written:

- `db/bossFights.ts:212` computes damage as `toRepEquivalent(resultValue, targetType)` —
  for `time`, seconds ÷ 3, no ceiling, no difficulty weight. A 60-minute walk is 1200 damage.
  The largest boss in the game has 1115 HP. **One walk one-shots the campaign**; The Golem
  dies in fourteen minutes of walking.
- `buildingLevelThresholds` maxes a building at 1000 units, and `getStyleVolumes` sums with no
  cap: 50 minutes of walking would max a volume-driven cardio building on day one, permanently.
  (Hypothetical in the strict sense — no cardio building exists in `buildingCodes` today, and
  approach B never creates a volume-driven one. The arithmetic is what matters.)
- XP survives only because that bug was already fixed once, in `db/xp.ts`
  (`effortCeilingSeconds`, `SPEED_ALLOWANCE`, `MAX_SESSION_XP`). Damage, village volume and
  oath volume never got the same treatment, because until now no exercise could log 3600
  seconds.
- **A per-activity rate cannot fix this.** The number would have to be small enough not to kill
  the campaign in one walk and large enough not to insult someone who walked an hour. No such
  number exists. The rate was the wrong knob; the unit is the knob.

It also flagged the exploit that fires by accident rather than by intent: `db/oaths.ts:219`
counts `COUNT(*) FROM completed_sessions` with no floor, so a free session you end whenever
turns `weekly_3x_8w` — the flagship eight-week oath — into three taps a week. See Open
Questions; this design does not close it.

Its OpenTracks suggestion (Apache-2.0 intent handshake instead of an in-house GPS module) was
considered as approach C and set aside, not refuted.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: the floor alone (chosen)
Chokepoint fix, dead tables deleted, three seed exercises, one screen, a leagues total in the
journal. Effort S (human ~2 d / CC ~0.5 d). It answers all three of the questions this session
started from; the road is what it defers, not the feature.

### Approach B: the floor plus the road
A, plus leagues driving a `road` building through the existing `TIER_4_DRIVERS`. Effort M
(human ~4 d / CC ~1 d). Chosen during the session, then **reversed after review**: the argument
for it was that a leagues counter with no building would be a "control wired to nothing", which
misreads that rule — it names a value *nothing* consumes, and a total rendered in the journal is
consumed by the reader. The road's real cost is about half a day across eight places, and its
floors would be guessed against league data that does not exist yet. It becomes v1.1.

### Approach C: the floor plus OpenTracks for distance
A or B, with distance coming from OpenTracks over intent + content provider instead of the native
module built on 2026-08-31. Deletes the Kotlin service, the battery tuning and the field test.
Set aside: it depends on an app the hero must install separately, and it abandons the
open-source proof that justified the GPS project. Worth reopening — and cheaper to reopen than
this doc first implied: what exists on disk from 2026-08-31 is an empty `modules/bati-location`
and its manifest, so C would delete work not yet done rather than work already paid for.

## Recommended Approach

**A — two currencies that cannot be exchanged, and the road deferred to v1.1.**

```
  cardio rows                          strength / calisthenics / yoga rows
       │                                              │
       │ raw seconds (later: metres)                  │ repEquivalentSql
       ▼                                              ▼
    LEAGUES ────► the road: the village's reach    REP-EQUIVALENTS ────► boss damage
       │                                              ├──► village volume
       └──► nothing else. Never damage,               ├──► muscle balance
            never village volume,                     └──► personal records
            never a rep.
```

A hero who only walks travels forever and kills nothing. A hero who only lifts hits very hard
at a wall they never reach. That tension is the reason a training app should want both, and it
is also the whole economic fix: cardio never reaches `toRepEquivalent`, so no boss is re-tuned
and no building threshold moves.

### The chokepoint, first and alone — and it is not one sentence
`toRepEquivalent` and `repEquivalentSql` are the single point every aggregate routes through.
They must return 0 for `cardio`. Three of the six call sites already have the style in scope
(`village.ts:284`, `completed.ts:498`, `muscleBalance.ts:116,295` all join `exercises`).
**Three do not**, and each needs its own answer:

- `oaths.ts:212` selects from `completed_exercises` filtered by `exerciseId`, with no join to
  `exercises`. Add the join.
- `bossFights.ts:212` takes `DamageParams`, which carries no `exerciseId` and is deliberately
  database-free ("the damage maths, with no database in it"). The style has to be threaded from
  the session store's in-memory hit preview, not looked up.
- `bossFights.ts:486` joins `quest_exercises` and `quests` only. Add `exercises`.

**`repEquivalentSql` cannot be made exhaustive.** It builds SQL from two columns and emits a
`CASE WHEN`; there is no TypeScript switch to exhaust, so the `never` default only ever
protects `toRepEquivalent`. The way to make premise 7's trap a compile error for both is to put
the per-type rule in one `Record<QuestTargetType, …>` and derive both functions from it — then a
new member of `questTargetTypes` fails `tsc` at the map, which is the "prefer a type over a
test" rule applied where it actually bites. `toRepEquivalent` also accepts
`QuestTargetType | null | undefined` and `DamageParams.targetType` is optional, so the null arm
needs handling explicitly or the default is unreachable and `tsc` stays quiet.

This ships **before** any cardio row can exist.

### Leagues
Their own query, deliberately not the style-volume one: `SUM(result_value)` over cardio rows.
**Name the unit before writing it.** Summing raw seconds today and raw metres later into one
column is precisely the unit-mixing bug `db/workUnits.ts` exists to fix, reintroduced one layer
up — a 60-minute walk (3600) and a 3 km run (3000) would land within a fifth of each other by
accident. Either two columns, or one conversion stated now and owned in one place, the way
`SECONDS_PER_REP_EQUIVALENT` is.

`getStyleVolumes` will read cardio as 0 forever by design. That deserves a comment where it is
computed, or someone will "fix" it. Note this leaves the tier-2 *style* slot (the one
`wizard_tower` and `druid_grove` fill) deliberately empty for cardio: the narrative hole and the
mechanical answer are in different places, on purpose.

### The road — v1.1, not v1
A new building whose driver is `leagues`, using the `{ driver, floors }` shape that
`TIER_4_DRIVERS` already has. Deferred so its floors can be chosen against real league totals
rather than a guess. Until it lands, leagues are a number the journal shows and nothing gates. What it upgrades is reach, not output — the only village
progression that is not a bigger number.

**Priced honestly after review: about half a day, not a line.** A new `BuildingCode` costs
`buildingCodes`, `buildingDefinitions`, `BUILDING_LABELS`, an icon in `constants/assetMap.ts`,
the `BuildingDriver` union, the hardcoded driver chain at `village.ts:331-336`,
`getBuildingProgress` (whose whitelist otherwise shows nothing for a locked road at level 0),
and a leagues fetch threaded into `LevelInputs` and the `Promise.all` in `getVillageBuildings`.

### The entry, and the two-second session it must not create
An ad-hoc quest: one round, one exercise, target optional.

**The clamp is the showstopper, found in review and verified.** `stores/session.ts:964` writes
`durationSeconds = Math.min(measuredSeconds, estimateQuestSeconds(quest) * 2)`. With no target,
`estimateExerciseSeconds` returns `Math.max(1, 0)` = 1 s, one round with one exercise adds no
rest, so a 25-minute walk is journalled as **2 seconds** — and `effortCeilingSeconds` then pays
it `XP_FLOOR`, 10 XP. Premise 1 ("inherits XP and the journal for free") and the first success
criterion are both false until this is handled. That ceiling exists to bound a runaway timer
against a known estimate; an expedition has no estimate to bound against, so an ad-hoc session
takes the measured seconds directly. The same question has to be answered for `setEffortSeconds`,
which credits `Math.min(done, target × 1.25)` — with no target that is the same ~100× penalty in
a second place. Decide what target an untargeted expedition hands the XP formula: it is not
zero, and it is not the elapsed time either without saying why.

Saying it "writes a normal `completed_sessions` row and touches nothing else" was too glib:
`saveSession` reads `quest.id`, `isDailyQuest(quest.id)`, `buildWarmup(quest, …)` and
`estimateQuestSeconds(quest)`, and `startSession` demands a full `Quest`. The design owes an
answer to whether the ad-hoc quest is a real `quests` row (simplest, pollutes the quest table)
or a synthetic object with a null-quest path through the store (cleaner, touches four call
sites). The suggested duration is presented as a
rumour rather than a plan ("the scouts say two hours' walk"), so clearing it is choosing to go
without knowing how far — the RPG justification for "no objective", at the cost of one string.

## Open Questions

1. **The session floor, left open by choice.** Leagues fix damage and the village; they do
   nothing for `COUNT(*) FROM completed_sessions`, so an expedition ended after ten seconds
   still counts toward `sessions` (`oaths.ts:219`) and `weekly_sessions`
   (`countQualifyingWeeks`, ~line 254) — which turns the flagship `weekly_3x_8w` oath into
   three taps a week. Review correction: "a session with no recorded work writes no row" does
   **not** solve it. `db/completed.ts:96` already throws on zero exercises, and an expedition
   always writes one row (the walk itself). The floor has to be on elapsed seconds or
   `result_value`, not on row count. And it is a deliberate guard with an error message, not
   the accident this doc first called it. Decide before the entry screen ships.
2. Names: the three activities, the resource (leagues?) and the building (road? gate? stable?).
3. Where the leagues total is read — journal, village, or both.
6. `app/oath.tsx`'s custom form offers any non-retired exercise, so a hero can swear an
   `exercise_volume` oath on a cardio movement and sit at 0 forever once the chokepoint returns
   0. Either exclude cardio from that picker for volume metrics, or give leagues an oath metric
   of their own.
7. ~~Do the three exercises declare `muscles` and a `pattern`?~~ **Settled 2026-08-31.**
   `pattern` is `locomotion` and `measure` is `time`; **no `exercise_muscles` rows at all**. A
   walk tagged `legs` would show the balance card a leg trained in N sessions for a volume of
   nothing. With no muscle rows the same card says something true instead — those results come
   from an exercise with no muscles set, so they are not counted there.
4. Distance: rides need it, and it arrives with the GPS work. The exhaustive switch must be in
   place first (premise 7).
5. The road's floors, in v1.1: league counts or something coarser, chosen against real totals
   rather than invented. This deferral is the reason the road is not in v1.

## Success Criteria

- A hero opens the app, taps once, walks 25 minutes and has a logged session, without choosing
  a quest and without a GPS fix.
- The three seed exercises exist with fantasy names and `style: cardio`.
- `toRepEquivalent`/`repEquivalentSql` return 0 for cardio, proven by a test, and adding a new
  `questTargetTypes` member without handling it fails `tsc`.
- Boss HP values and `buildingLevelThresholds` are untouched by this change.
- `goals`, `goal_progress` and `scheduled_sessions` are gone, migration included.
- A leagues total is visible and correct after an expedition. (The road it will eventually
  drive is v1.1 and is not a criterion here.)

## Distribution Plan

Existing pipeline: `npm run release`, `release.yml`, F-Droid recipe. Nothing new. The dead-table
migration must be forward-only like every other one, and `db/backup.ts` never mentioned those
tables, so no backup format changes.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You did not ask for a feature, you asked three questions and let the answers arrive in any
  order — then added "ça peut être un nombre de kilomètres" and "trouver aussi une
  justification RPG" mid-flight, which is how the design got its two best parts.
- Asking for the RPG justification is what turned this from a stopwatch into a design. The hole
  in the village was already in the schema; it took someone asking "why would this exist in the
  world" to go look for it.
- You accepted eight premises without argument and then reversed your own earlier answer when
  the arithmetic contradicted it. That is the rarer of the two.
