# UX audit, 2026-09-10

48 screenshots, 36 of a hero with three years of history and 12 of a fresh install, captured by
[`npm run audit:shots`](../../../scripts/audit-shots.sh) on a Fairphone 6, English, v2.2.0. Four
personas judged them independently, each arguing for its own user and nobody else's, against
[`ui-checklist.md`](../ui-checklist.md), [`writing.md`](../../product/writing.md) and
[`PRODUCT.md`](../../../PRODUCT.md). 55 findings came back, merged here.

Full reports: [the stranger](2026-09-10-stranger.md) (never lifted, never played an RPG),
[the hurried lifter](2026-09-10-hurried-lifter.md) (in the gym, forty seconds of rest),
[the player](2026-09-10-player.md) (came for the game),
[the regular](2026-09-10-regular.md) (trains daily, opens the journal to see himself improve).
[references.md](references.md) is the scout's pass over the best sport apps, used as ammunition
by the last two.

---

## 1. Confirmed in the code

Four findings were checked against the source rather than left as readings of a picture. All four
are fixed, in #74, with tests; what follows is what they were, because the diagnosis is the
reusable part.

**The Stats tab adds up a hundred sessions and calls it a total.**
[`app/(tabs)/journal/index.tsx:122`](<../../../app/(tabs)/journal/index.tsx>) calls
`listCompletedSessions(100)`, and `JournalStats` derives every figure from that array. Home reads
`count()` over the whole table. So a hero with 547 sessions is shown "100 Total Workouts",
"2908 Total Minutes", and a Difficulty Split that sums to exactly 100. Every "Total" on that
screen is a 100-row window over three years. **Bug.** The aggregates belong in SQL next to the
existing `count()`; the list keeps its page.

**Closing a village building sheet with the hardware back button stops the app answering taps.**
Two of the four capture runs died on it: after `village-detail` closes, `tab-journal` is found in
the hierarchy and tapped, the tap registers, and nothing navigates. Six screenshots in a row came
out as the previous screen, twice, and only a full restart recovered.

[`.maestro/village-sheet-check.yaml`](../../../.maestro/village-sheet-check.yaml) tries all three
ways a person can close that sheet and photographs the Journal tab after each. The answer is
narrow:

| Closed with | Journal opens after |
|---|---|
| Hardware back | **No.** The app lands on Home and stops responding to the tab bar |
| The sheet's own Close button | Yes |
| A tap on the scrim | Yes |

The back press is being handled twice: the sheet closes *and* the router pops, which is why the
app is on Home rather than on the village it was showing. So the village screen unmounts while
the sheet is still animating out, and what it leaves behind eats every tap. That also makes it a
plain navigation bug on its own, one gesture doing two things.

**Bug, and the highest-priority one here**: nothing else on this list stops the app, and Android
back is a primary gesture, not an edge case. The fix is to consume the back press while the sheet
is open so the router never sees it.

**The same quest advertises two durations and two rewards.** The gallery card says "~ 12 min,
up to +140 XP", its own detail says "~ 10 min, up to +70 XP" at the same Medium level, Home says
"~ 10 min" and no XP. **Bug**, and the shape of it is known:
[`AGENTS.md`](../../../AGENTS.md) § Known debt already records that the quest screen composes the
config by hand instead of calling `loadConfiguredQuest()`, and warns that the two must read the
same saved config.

**Session details does not open.** The newest history row reads "Quest not found" with a "--"
duration, and tapping it lands on "Something went wrong, Session not found, Retry". Seen on
seeded data, so the seed may be the cause rather than the app, but the screen's behaviour is a
finding either way: a session whose quest was deleted still has a date, a duration, a difficulty
and its logged exercises. **Bug.** It must degrade, not error.

## 3. Friction

Grouped by what they are about, because several are the same wound seen from two sides.

**The session screen, where the hurried lifter lives**

- "Replace" and "I couldn't do this one" sit one line above the full-width Done button, in the landing zone of a thumb that hammers Done between sets. Move them into the pause overlay, which already has room. *(hurried)*
- Two unlabelled progress bars compete for a glance at the top, and neither is the hold in progress. Turn the second into the hold's own progress. *(hurried)*
- "Skip warm-up" is the smallest, dimmest text on a screen whose biggest element is a countdown, and nothing says how long the whole warm-up runs. *(hurried)*
- The exercise plates are the one place the art leaves dark fantasy: brightly lit studio photography, smiling figures, and an ambient tint that turns the lower half of the screen khaki for the duration of the set. *(player)*
- Reading the how-to costs a pause and a modal that repeats the art already on screen, with "Quit Quest" visible through the scrim. *(hurried)*

**Choosing what to do**

- Home's one filled button opens a page about the workout instead of starting it, though the card already carries everything needed to decide. *(hurried)*
- Finding the three movements in a quest costs a scroll past image, title, flavour, four chips and the level row, and each movement is then a card a third of a screen tall. *(hurried)*
- A level 44 hero is offered the tutorial campaign first, five empty dashes and "+63 XP per step" for a hero who earns three times that per session. Sort by threat, not catalogue order. *(player)*
- A five-chapter campaign is a settings list: 80px thumbnails, the sentence "Finish the previous step to unlock it" repeated verbatim four times, under 450px of cover art. *(player)*
- The adventure offer is raised on entry to a quest screen, with the loud button leading away from the quest just chosen. *(hurried)*

**Progress that cannot be read**

- "Your Progress" charts attendance only, sessions and minutes, with deltas that name no baseline and bars with no values. A week of hard singles and a week of easy volume draw the same picture. *(regular)*
- A history row gives duration and difficulty, never what was done in it. Two runs of the same quest a month apart are indistinguishable. *(regular)*
- The level bar says 0% toward something it never names, while the lifetime XP total sits elsewhere on the same screen in a different unit of account. *(regular)*
- The streak card's only comparison is the number against itself, "1092, best 1092", and it never states the quota that keeps it alive. *(regular)*
- Every ready-made oath is a target already passed years ago, and no row shows where the hero stands against it. *(regular, player)*
- The recap's one comparison, "Best league, 4:35 /km", does not say whether that is this outing, this route, or a lifetime best, and a named route says nothing about the previous times on it. *(regular)*
- Village progress is denominated in "work units", a currency that exists nowhere else and converts to nothing. *(regular, player)*

**The village**

- The painting is finished at level 44 and six buildings are still at three of five, so the remaining progress lands in a grid of meters beside a scene that can never change again. The two progress systems never look at each other. *(player)*
- The building sheet's dots and bar are two different progress readings on the same card, neither labelled, and the one explanatory sentence sits under the wrong heading. *(stranger)*

**First contact**

- The three level options look less pressable than the greyed-out primary below them, nothing is preselected, and "Regular" means nothing to someone who has never trained. *(stranger)*
- The village-name step ends with the same sentence as the button on the previous screen, in flat grey, so it reads as a stalled app. *(stranger)*
- The clearest screen in the app, the first-session offer, still will not name the four movements it is about to ask for. *(stranger)*
- Four onboarding screens with no way back. The top-left corner is empty on all of them. *(stranger)*
- A cameo covers the whole second card on the Adventures tab, carrying the most useful sentence on the screen, with no visible dismiss. *(stranger)*
- Home's pinned village strip covers a row of the page underneath it, and calls the village "Hamlet" when the hero named it something else. *(stranger)*
- "Head out" offers three cards with play triangles, the universal sign for "this starts now", with no duration, no distance, and no line saying these happen outdoors. *(stranger)*
- The resume card is styled as an error, amber and a warning triangle, outranks the session that just ended, and never says when the session was abandoned. *(player, hurried)*

## 4. Polish

Kept short; the detail is in the four reports.

- Two gear icons in Home's top bar, same shape, same grey, twelve centimetres apart. *(stranger)*
- The Journal's two views are identical when empty, with no visible segmented control naming them, and the empty state's cartoon scroll emoji is from a different art world than everything around it. *(stranger)*
- "I already have a backup" is offered on the first screen of a fresh install, a state the reader cannot be in, in a word about files. *(stranger)*
- The Difficulty Split flattens three years into one bar with no time axis, so the one thing it could prove, that the hero moved up, is the one thing it cannot show. *(regular)*
- Career totals, 547 quests and 67.0k XP, take a row in the main column of the screen opened between sets. *(hurried, player)*
- The oath list flattens four different kinds of promise into eight identical pills with the same icon and chevron. *(player)*
- The maxed village never admits it is maxed, and the one thread left, a building that stopped rising, carries no number. *(regular)*
- The weekly target the flame is judged against is not in Preferences, though it decides whether the streak survives the week. *(regular)*

## 4b. Where a persona read the picture wrong

Two findings were right about the wound and wrong about the cause, which is what a still image
costs. Both are recorded because the fix changed as a result.

- **The session timer counts down, not up.** "0:24" is what is left of a 30 second hold, and the
  bar under the movement's name does track it. What made it read as an elapsed count is the copy
  around it: the unit "Seconds" under the number, and "Keep going! Timer continues after target"
  under that, a sentence about overtime shown before the target is reached. The finding stands,
  the fix is a caption rather than a new readout.
- **The second progress bar is the hold.** The friction list says neither of the session
  screen's two bars tracks the set in progress. The thin one at the top is the quest's, and the
  thicker one under the movement's name is `useSessionTimer`'s own `progress`: at 0:24 left of 30
  it sits at a fifth, and in the boss shot at over half because 0:12 was the *elapsed* reading of
  a longer hold. What was true is that neither was labelled, which the caption under the numeral
  now answers.
- **Reading the movement pauses the clock on purpose.** "Costs a pause" is the trade the code
  made deliberately, with the reason written beside it: a hero who does not know what a dead bug
  is should not be charged seconds for asking. What was true is the rest of that finding, that
  the paused screen's "Quit Quest" was legible through the modal's scrim.
- **The cameo layer is not `pointerEvents="none"` where it draws.** The comment in `screenCues.ts`
  says the layer never intercepts, and the container is `box-none`, but the figure and the bubble
  are `Pressable` on purpose, so a villager you can see can be sent away. The persona wrote "I
  cannot tell from the shot whether he eats the tap or only hides it". He ate it.

## 5. Where the personas disagree

These are design decisions, not bugs, and they are the interesting output of running four readers
instead of one.

**How much world belongs inside a session.** The player wants the story present where the hero
actually is, and calls the narrative dialog a permission prompt. The hurried lifter wants nothing
between the tap and the first rep and calls the same dialog an interruption. Both are right about
the placement and wrong about each other's need. The scout's third finding resolves it: the beat
belongs in the ninety seconds of `RestView`, where the hero is already waiting, rather than
before the quest starts or after it ends.

**Who owns the top of the boss screen.** The player wants the monster to be dangerous and
present; the hurried lifter wants the movement's name to be the biggest thing on screen and
cannot find it under the dragon. Resolution: the boss keeps the moment of the kill, full screen,
and loses the permanent band, capped to a strip of art, name and HP while a set is running.

**What Home is for.** The hurried lifter wants one button that starts the session. The regular
wants Home to show progress worth opening the app for. The player wants a superlative that can be
beaten this week. The stranger wants to be told what to do. All four agree on one thing: the
career totals row serves none of them.

## 6. Where to start

> **Status, 2026-09-11.** Ten of the twelve are done: the four bugs in #74, fixes 5 to 8 in #75,
> #78 and #79, the movement records of 11 in #76, the stats pass in #77, the recap of 12 in #81,
> and the boss readout of 9 in #82. Everything after #75 was looked at on a device before it
> landed, per § 4.
>
> Eleven of twelve now: the PR badge of 11 landed in #83, storing the records on the row
> (`0051`), which is the trade the product chose over a query per row on a scrolling list.
>
> One is open, and it is a decision rather than a task. **The other half of 10:** the narrative
> modal lost its dialog chrome in #75, and the confirmation stays where it is, by decision. Moving
> the beat into the rest window is a different change, because that modal is also what starts an
> adventure step, "Begin Adventure" against "Not now". What could belong in `RestView` is a beat
> that is not a gate, and nobody has written one.
>
> Not attempted: the boss's live readout still shows the campaign pool rather than tonight's
> contribution (9's fourth part), which needs the session's starting HP threaded into the arena.

Four bugs, four fixes, four redesigns. The bugs are first because they are cheap, confirmed, and
one of them stops the app. Nothing here is a mockup: per
[`audit-protocol.md`](../audit-protocol.md) § 4 a redesign is built on a branch and re-photographed
by the same flow, and the same four personas judge before against after.

**Bugs, in order.**

1. The village sheet leaves the app deaf on hardware back, and only on hardware back. Consume the
   back press while the sheet is open, so it closes the sheet and stops there instead of also
   popping the village screen out from under it. Re-run
   [`.maestro/village-sheet-check.yaml`](../../../.maestro/village-sheet-check.yaml) after: all
   three shots must show the journal.
2. `listCompletedSessions(100)` feeding every "Total" tile. Move the aggregates into SQL beside
   the `count()` Home already uses. The list keeps its page.
3. One quest, one duration, one reward. The gallery card and the detail must read the same saved
   config, which is `loadConfiguredQuest()`, the debt already written down in `AGENTS.md`.
4. Session details degrades instead of erroring. A session whose quest was deleted still has a
   date, a duration, a difficulty and its exercises.

**Fixes, in order of how many hero-days they cost today.**

5. A cameo never intersects a control. One rule in the cameo layer: it enters above the action
   zone or over art, never over a CTA, a chip row or a card's only content, and a tap anywhere
   dismisses it. This is three findings across three screens, not one.
6. Print the target next to the count on the session screen, "0:23 / 30s", and make the second
   progress bar the hold's own. Nothing else on that screen moves.
7. The answer to the level question reaches the quest list, or the question stops being asked.
   A "For you" row at the top is enough; a difficulty word on each card is better.
8. The first session survives onboarding. Home's card carries the workout that was just accepted,
   its minutes and its movement count, and the button says "Start", with "Pick a quest" demoted
   to a link.

**Redesigns, each needing a branch and a before-and-after verdict.**

9. The boss readout: tonight's contribution rather than the campaign pool, the resistance and the
   crit odds in words under the counter, the HP out of the "you are fine" colour, and the band
   capped to a strip while a set runs so the movement keeps the title.
10. The narrative beat moves into the rest window, where the hero is already waiting, and loses
    its dialog chrome. This is the one change both the player and the hurried lifter want, for
    opposite reasons.
11. Records per movement, replacing the six global session records that stop being beatable in
    month one, and the history's "PR" badge naming what it broke. The scout's first two findings
    are the shape of this.
12. The expedition recap pays in leagues and says what they moved, above the distance and the
    pace. The tile-server paragraph goes behind the button it is actually about.

## 6b. Three surfaces audited on their own

The owner named three places that felt wrong and asked for a designer's pass on each. They read
the same shots, plus the source of the screens no screenshot covers, and each marks which of its
findings came from code rather than from a picture.

- [Starting a session, and setting up an outing](2026-09-10-home-and-outings.md) - the number of
  moves between opening the app and being under way, for a quest and for a walk.
- [The outing and its trace](2026-09-10-outings.md) - what the hero sees while moving, what the
  phone already knows and does not show, and where the drawing of the route stops.
- [The stats tab](2026-09-10-stats.md) - which cards earn their place, in what order, and what the
  existing data would already support.
